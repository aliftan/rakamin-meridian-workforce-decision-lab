# Model and Evidence Notes

## Provenance categories

- **Case brief:** ~52,000 total employees, ~6,000 Field Metering / Manual Operations, an estimated 70% automatable workload over an 18-month horizon; no mass layoffs; existing technology procurements and systems constraints.
- **Illustrative synthetic inputs:** allocation to 12 job families and R1–R6, exposure intervals outside Field Metering, assumed contractor opportunities, readiness, attrition, receiving-work capacity, rollout sequences, and monetary rates.
- **Model-derived outputs:** simulated exposed-work equivalents, month-specific gaps, required receiving-work capacity, and sensitivity results. These are not observed outcomes or confirmed headcount vacancies.

## AHA capacity comparison

At month 18, a simplified capacity requirement is:

```
Realized displaced work         3,570 FTE-equivalents
Less relevant attrition           225 FTE-equivalents
Net requiring absorption        3,345 FTE-equivalents
Illustrative matching readiness  65%
Required receiving-work capacity 3,345 / 0.65 ≈ 5,146
Assumed receiving-work capacity  900 (or 660 without contractor option)
```

This is a **planning threshold under assumed inputs**, not a verified opportunity count. Temporary assignments and work redesign require separately evidenced workloads and cannot automatically be counted as permanent destinations.

## Financial interpretation

The illustrative program economics model cash savings chiefly through avoidable contractor substitution, which the brief does **not** establish. At 36 months, 3,900 contractor FTE-months × Rp6 million would produce Rp23.4 billion *if* those costs could be avoided; against Rp6 billion costs, undiscounted ROI would be +290%. With zero contractor savings and **no other modeled cash benefit categories**, ROI is −100%. Neither case is a validated business forecast.

Other possible cash categories (e.g., recruitment avoidance, overtime avoidance) need independent evidence and Finance sign-off. Non-contractor productive value is not cash savings. A nominal Rp600 million illustrative delay cost is not necessarily an incremental cash payment. Gateable scenario NPV is intentionally not presented because month-by-month cash flow rephasing has not been fully modelled.

## Limitations to disclose during demo

1. The app uses interpolated curves anchored to the previous synthetic scenario model, not all Excel formulas recomputed dynamically.
2. Distinct regional distributions and job-family exposure intervals are **deliberately synthetic**, not Client findings.
3. Data confidence is lower for job families beyond Field Metering; exposure does not imply automatic redundancy or a near-term transition need.
4. Matching requires validated receiving work, consent and task-specific evidence, independent review, safety constraints and human decisions.
5. If contractor demand is zero and rollout cannot be gated, the appropriate response is **adjust and escalate**: redesign productive work, evaluate feasible mobility and attrition, implement safe interim assignments, and request Board decisions. Do not promise full absorption.
6. The *least-confident strategic decision* is choosing demand-led redeployment as the primary bet while receiving-work demand remains unverified; validate Operations workload and Finance contractor spend within 30 days.
